CHAPTER 01

Matter in Our Surroundings

NCERT Chapter 1

1. Physical Nature of Matter & States

Everything in the universe that has mass and occupies space is called Matter. Matter is made up of tiny particles with intermolecular space and attractive forces between them.

  • Solid: Fixed shape and volume, high density, negligible compressibility.
  • Liquid: Fixed volume, no fixed shape, fluid, moderate compressibility.
  • Gas: Neither fixed shape nor volume, high kinetic energy, highly compressible.

2. Latent Heat & Evaporation

Latent Heat: The hidden heat energy required to change the state of a substance without changing its temperature.

  • Latent Heat of Fusion: Heat needed to convert 1 kg of solid into liquid at atmospheric pressure at its melting point.
  • Latent Heat of Vaporisation: Heat required to convert 1 kg of liquid into gas at its boiling point.
  • Evaporation: Surface phenomenon causing cooling. Rate increases with surface area, temperature, and wind speed, but decreases with humidity.
Solved Practice Numerical: Temperature Conversion

Problem: Convert 300 K to Celsius scale and 25°C to Kelvin scale.

Solution: Temperature in °C = K - 273 = 300 - 273 = 27°C.

Solution: Temperature in K = °C + 273 = 25 + 273 = 298 K.

CHAPTER 02

Is Matter Around Us Pure?

NCERT Chapter 2

1. Pure Substances vs Mixtures

Pure Substance: Consists of a single type of particle (e.g., pure Gold, Water).

Mixture: Contains two or more pure substances mixed physically.

  • Homogeneous Mixture: Uniform composition throughout (e.g., Salt in water).
  • Heterogeneous Mixture: Non-uniform composition (e.g., Sand in water, Oil & Water).

2. True Solutions, Colloids & Suspensions

  • Solution: Homogeneous, particle size < 1 nm, does not scatter light.
  • Colloid: Heterogeneous appearance but stable, particle size 1-100 nm, exhibits Tyndall Effect.
  • Suspension: Heterogeneous, particle size > 100 nm, unstable, particles settle down.
Solved Concentration Numerical:

Problem: A solution contains 40 g of common salt in 320 g of water. Calculate concentration in mass percentage.

Mass of Solution = Solute (40g) + Solvent (320g) = 360 g.

Mass % = (40 / 360) × 100 = 11.11% (w/w).

CHAPTER 03

Atoms & Molecules

NCERT Chapter 3

1. Laws of Chemical Combination

Law of Conservation of Mass: Mass can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. (Lavoisier)

Law of Constant Proportions: In a chemical substance, elements are always present in definite proportions by mass. (Proust)

2. Mole Concept & Formulae

1 Mole = 6.022 × 1023 particles (Avogadro Constant $N_A$).

Number of moles (n) = Given Mass (m) / Molar Mass (M)

Number of moles (n) = Given Particles (N) / 6.022 × 1023

Solved Mole Concept Numerical:

Problem: Calculate the number of moles in 52 g of Helium (He, atomic mass = 4 u).

n = Given Mass / Molar Mass = 52 / 4 = 13 moles of He.

CHAPTER 04

Structure of the Atom

NCERT Chapter 4

1. Subatomic Particles & Models

  • Electrons (e-): Discovered by J.J. Thomson (Cathode Rays).
  • Protons (p+): Discovered by E. Goldstein (Anode Rays).
  • Neutrons (n0): Discovered by J. Chadwick (Neutral particle in nucleus).
  • Rutherford Model: Alpha particle scattering experiment proved nucleus existence.
  • Bohr's Model: Electrons revolve in discrete energy orbits (K, L, M, N) without radiating energy.

2. Electronic Distribution & Isotopes

Bohr-Bury Scheme: Maximum electrons in nth shell = $2n^2$. K shell = 2, L shell = 8, M shell = 18.

Valency: Combining capacity of an atom determined by outermost shell electrons.

Isotopes: Same Atomic Number (Z), Different Mass Number (A) (e.g., Protium, Deuterium, Tritium).

Isobars: Same Mass Number (A), Different Atomic Number (Z) (e.g., Calcium and Argon both have A=40).

Solved Average Atomic Mass Numerical:

Problem: Chlorine occurs in nature as 75% Cl-35 and 25% Cl-37. Find average atomic mass.

Average Mass = (35 × 75/100) + (37 × 25/100) = 26.25 + 9.25 = 35.5 u.
