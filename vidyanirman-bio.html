CHAPTER ONE

The Cell: Fundamental Unit of Life

Historical Milestones & Discovery

The cell is the basic structural, functional, and biological unit of all known living organisms. In 1665, Robert Hooke observed dead cork cells under a primitive microscope, coining the term "cell" from the Latin cellula (meaning small room).

1665 — Robert Hooke

Discovered dead cork cells (cell wall remnants).

1674 — Anton van Leeuwenhoek

Observed first living cells (free living algae, bacteria in pond water).

1831 — Robert Brown

Discovered and named the Nucleus inside orchid cells.

1838–39 — Schleiden & Schwann

Formulated the Cell Theory (all plants & animals composed of cells).

1855 — Rudolf Virchow

Expanded cell theory with "Omnis cellula-e cellula" (All living cells arise from pre-existing cells).

Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic

PROKARYOTES (e.g. Bacteria, Anabaena)
  • Size: Generally small (1-10 µm).
  • Undefined nuclear region (Nucleoid lacking nuclear membrane).
  • Single circular chromosome; membrane-bound organelles absent.
EUKARYOTES (e.g. Plants, Animals, Fungi)
  • Size: Generally larger (5-100 µm).
  • Well-defined nuclear region enclosed by nuclear envelope.
  • Multiple linear chromosomes & membrane-bound organelles present.

Nucleus (Master Controller)

Double-membrane bound organelle housing DNA in chromatin form. Directs all cellular metabolism, protein synthesis, and cell division.

  • Enclosed by nuclear membrane with pore complexes.
  • Contains nucleolus (ribosome subunit factory).
  • Houses genetic information in chromosomes (DNA + Proteins).
Key Analogy: "The Brain / City Hall of the Cell"

Mitochondria

Known as the Powerhouse of the Cell. Generates ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate) via cellular respiration. Features a double membrane (inner folded into cristae) and contains its own DNA and 70S ribosomes!

Plastids (Chloroplasts)

Found in plant cells. Includes Chloroplasts (green chlorophyll-containing plastids for photosynthesis), Chromoplasts (colored pigment holders), and Leucoplasts (white/colorless storage for starch, oils).

Lysosomes

Termed "Suicidal Bags of the Cell". Membrane-bound sacs packed with powerful hydrolytic digestive enzymes (made by RER). Digests cellular waste, worn-out organelles, or foreign invaders via autolysis.

Endoplasmic Reticulum & Golgi

RER (Rough ER with ribosomes) synthesizes proteins. SER (Smooth ER) synthesizes lipids and detoxifies poisons. Golgi Apparatus modifies, packages, and dispatches materials in vesicles.

CHAPTER TWO

Tissues: Division of Labor

Plant Tissues Master Hierarchy

A tissue is a group of cells similar in structure performing a specific collective function. Plant tissues are divided based on dividing capacity into Meristematic Tissues and Permanent Tissues.

Meristematic Tissues

Actively dividing young cells with thin cellulose walls, dense cytoplasm, and prominent nuclei. Lacks vacuoles.

  • Apical Meristem: Present at growing tips of stems & roots. Increases length (primary growth).
  • Intercalary Meristem: Located at base of leaves/internodes (e.g. grass stems).
  • Lateral Meristem (Cambium): Located on lateral sides. Increases girth/girth diameter (secondary growth).
Simple Permanent Tissues

Composed of only one type of structurally similar cells performing protective/support functions.

  • Parenchyma: Thin-walled live cells. Unspecialized storage. Modified as Chlorenchyma (photosynthesis) & Aerenchyma (buoyancy in aquatic plants).
  • Collenchyma: Living cells thickened at corners with pectin. Provides mechanical flexibility to stems/petioles.
  • Sclerenchyma: Dead, narrow cells with thick lignified walls. Provides husk strength (e.g. coconut husk).
Complex Permanent Tissues

Composed of multiple cell types working together as a vascular unit for long-distance transport.

  • Xylem: Conducts water & minerals unidirectionally (upward). Components: Tracheids, Vessels, Xylem Parenchyma, Xylem Fibers.
  • Phloem: Translocates food (sucrose) bidirectionally. Components: Sieve Tubes, Companion Cells, Phloem Parenchyma, Phloem Fibers.

Animal Tissues Classification

1. Epithelial Tissue (Covering & Protection)

Tightly packed continuous sheets covering organs and lining body cavities.

  • • Squamous: Extremely thin, flat cells (lining of mouth, blood vessels, alveoli).
  • • Cuboidal: Cube-like cells providing mechanical support (kidney tubules, salivary ducts).
  • • Columnar: Tall pillar-like cells aiding absorption & secretion (intestine lining).
  • • Ciliated: Columnar cells with hair-like cilia (respiratory tract to push mucus).

2. Connective Tissue (Binding & Support)

Cells loosely spaced and embedded in an intercellular matrix (fluid, jelly, or rigid).

  • • Blood: Fluid matrix (plasma) with RBCs, WBCs, and Platelets.
  • • Bone & Cartilage: Bone (hard calcium/phosphorus matrix); Cartilage (solid sugar/protein matrix in nose, ear, trachea).
  • • Ligaments & Tendons: Ligaments connect Bone to Bone; Tendons connect Muscle to Bone.
  • • Areolar & Adipose: Areolar fills space between organs; Adipose stores fat below skin.

3. Muscular Tissue (Movement & Contraction)

Elongated contractile protein fibers (actin & myosin) causing muscle contraction.

  • • Striated / Skeletal: Voluntary, cylindrical, multinucleate with light/dark bands.
  • • Smooth / Unstriated: Involuntary, spindle-shaped, uninucleate (stomach, iris, bronchi).
  • • Cardiac: Involuntary, branched, uninucleate with intercalated discs (heart walls).

4. Nervous Tissue (Signal Transmission)

Specialized cells called Neurons responding to stimuli and conducting nerve impulses.

  • • Cell Body (Cyton): Contains nucleus & cytoplasm with Nissl granules.
  • • Dendrites: Short branched processes receiving incoming electrical signals.
  • • Axon & Synapse: Long single axon insulated by myelin sheath transmitting impulses to terminal synapse.

CHAPTER THREE

Reproduction & Cell Division

Asexual Reproduction Modes

Single-parent reproduction producing genetically identical offspring (clones) without gamete fusion.

Binary & Multiple Fission: Amoeba splits into 2 identical cells; Plasmodium undergoes multiple fission inside cyst.
Budding: Outgrowth (bud) arises on parent body (Hydra, Yeast), detaches when mature.
Fragmentation & Regeneration: Spirogyra breaks into fragments; Planaria regenerates complete body from cut pieces.
Spore Formation & Vegetative Propagation: Rhizopus produces sporangia spores; Bryophyllum propagates via leaf notch buds.

Mitosis vs Meiosis Comparison

Cell division is the mechanism for growth, tissue repair, and gamete formation in multicellular organisms.

Feature Mitosis (Equational) Meiosis (Reductional)
Location Somatic (body) cells Germ cells (gonads)
Daughter Cells 2 Identical (2n) 4 Non-identical (1n)
Chromosomes Same as parent (Diploid) Halved (Haploid)
Purpose Growth & Repair Gamete formation

Angiosperms: Monocotyledons vs Dicotyledons

Monocots (Monocotyledons)
  • Single cotyledon seed leaf (e.g. Wheat, Maize, Rice, Grass).
  • Parallel leaf venation.
  • Fibrous root system.
  • Trimerous floral whorls (multiples of 3).
Dicots (Dicotyledons)
  • Two cotyledon seed leaves (e.g. Gram, Pea, Mango, Rose).
  • Reticulate (net-like) leaf venation.
  • Taproot system.
  • Tetramerous or Pentamerous floral whorls (multiples of 4 or 5).
